1. Introduction & dataset

The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) is the CDC’s premier system of health-related telephone surveys, collecting state-level data on U.S. residents’ health-related risk behaviors, chronic conditions, and use of preventive services across all 50 states, the District of Columbia, and three territories, with more than 400,000 adult interviews per year (course guidelines, §1). This project uses the BRFSS 2021 LLCP public file: 438,693 respondent records × 303 columns, all numeric-encoded (EXP-000; results/profiling/column_census.csv). The analysis target is GENHLTH, the five-level self-rated health item. Self-rated general health is a meaningful prediction target because it is one of the most widely used summary indicators in survey epidemiology — the CDC itself publishes an official derived indicator from it — and predicting it from behavioral, functional, and socio-demographic factors is a technically demanding instance of learning from wide, noisy, sentinel-coded survey data.

The dataset poses four documented challenges, all confirmed empirically in EXP-000:

  1. High dimensionality with heavy missingness. 92 of 303 columns exceed 90% blank+sentinel; 155 exceed 50%; 3 are completely empty; 247 contain at least one sentinel code (EXP-000).
  2. Sentinel-coded missingness. Don’t-know/refused values (7/77/777, 9/99/999, and 6-digit variants) and codebook-specific “none” codes (88) are stored as ordinary numbers and must be recoded before modeling.
  3. Class imbalance. Under the chosen binary framing, 16.63% of valid rows are positive (Table 1).
  4. Variables directly related to the target. Calculated columns derived from GENHLTH (notably _RFHLTH) would leak the label; they are identified and blacklisted before modeling (EXP-000; §2–§3).

2. Task framing — binary classification per ADR-001

Target definition (ADR-001). y = 1 iff GENHLTH ∈ {4, 5} (Fair or Poor); y = 0 iff GENHLTH ∈ {1, 2, 3}. Rows with GENHLTH ∈ {7, 9, blank} are dropped, never imputed (1,161 rows; EXP-000). The metric is ROC-AUC and the validation scheme is stratified 10-fold CV on identical folds for every model (AGENTS.md; fold SHA 3b3a17457039 asserted in all 41 model-experiment rows of results/experiments_log.csv; EXP-000, the profiling row, has no CV folds by design).

Why this cut, and why it is not arbitrary. The CDC itself derives its official “adults with fair or poor health” indicator _RFHLTH from GENHLTH with exactly this boundary. Our binary target therefore mirrors an established operationalization used in federal reporting rather than a convenience split (ADR-001). A direct empirical consequence: anything derived from GENHLTH is target leakage. The leakage probe (EXP-000; results/profiling/leakage_probe.csv) confirmed this in the strongest possible terms — _RFHLTH agrees with our y on 437,532 of 437,532 valid rows (100.0% agreement, perfectly diagonal crosstab). In the 2021 coding, _RFHLTH = 2 means Fair/Poor (y = 1); the reversed mapping agrees on zero rows. For contrast, GENHLTH itself shows Spearman ρ = +0.672 against y (EXP-000). The leak columns GENHLTH, _RFHLTH, _PHYS14D, _MENT14D were therefore hard-excluded from the feature matrix (ADR-004/ADR-005), and the matrix is built by whitelist, so a drop-list typo can never leak the target (§3).

A subtler leakage question was resolved with data rather than decree (ADR-004). The Tier-3 “construct-suspicion” block — the health-days items PHYSHLTH (ρ = −0.293 pre-recode), MENTHLTH (ρ = −0.099), POORHLTH (ρ = −0.104, 50.8% coverage) — is strongly associated with the target, but none is derived from GENHLTH; each measures a distinct construct (days of poor physical/mental health; days of activity limitation). The contrast case is instructive: _RFHLTH’s 100% agreement is what true target derivation looks like; ρ ≈ 0.3 is what a legitimately strong predictor looks like. All three were kept, with POORHLTH’s final fate left to empirical feature selection (ADR-004).

Alternatives considered and rejected (ADR-001). Poor (5) vs rest — 4.1% positives (18,005 rows; Table 1): harsher imbalance, noisier fold scores, thin error-analysis pool; rejected. Keep the native 5-class problem as the main task — contradicts the course’s explicit binary framing and makes the required ROC-AUC/Dummy baseline awkward; rejected, with the optional multiclass extension explicitly declined in ADR-002 (scope: Basic + Intermediate + Advanced via boosting libraries, automated search, and nested CV instead). Good-or-better vs rest — identical partition with flipped labels; no distinct experiment.

Consequences of the framing. A 16.63% positive rate gives stable stratified folds (~7.3k positives per fold) and a genuinely exercised imbalance question — answered empirically in §6 — while the ordinal 3|4 boundary being binarized turns out to be the dominant error mode, analyzed in §9.

3. Data cleaning & preprocessing decisions

Preprocessing is governed by one principle from the Leakage watchlist: the feature matrix is built from an explicit keep-list, never by blacklisting drops — a typo in a drop-list must not leak the target. Raw data are immutable; the cleaned matrix is checkpointed once to data/interim/clean_v1.parquet (438,693 × 76, sentinels → NaN, engineering recodes applied, no target column) so that no CV fold ever re-cleans (ADR-005).

Whitelist construction: 303 → 76 columns via seven rules (ADR-005). Every one of the 303 census columns received an explicit verdict and reason in results/profiling/triage_decisions.csv — no silent drops. Rules applied with precedence R1 > R3 > R2 > R6 > R4 > R5 > keep: R1 leakage blacklist (GENHLTH, _RFHLTH, _PHYS14D, _MENT14D; enforced by a hard assert in src/preprocessing.py); R2 identifiers/admin/weights/telephone-flow (SEQNO, _PSU, _LLCPWT, _STSTR, MSA/landline/cell admin, NUMHHOL3, CPDEMO1B, MSCODE); R3 census-constant columns (11 constants + IYEAR); R4 sparse micro-coverage modules (>90% blank+sentinel; per ADR-004 this includes the caregiver block CDASSIST/CDHOUSE/CDSOCIAL, whose |ρ| = 0.28–0.32 on ~1% coverage was a selection artifact); R5 a coverage gate — survival requires ≥25% effective coverage after sentinel recode, 10–25% → module drop (borderline cases resolved by the rule: PREDIAB1 25.98% kept, PDIABTST 24.86% dropped, SOMALE 25.23% the minimum kept); R6 duplicate-resolution with keep-raw preference (e.g. _BMI5 over the height/weight/BMI-flag family, _SMOKER3 over SMOKE100/SMOKDAY2/_RFSMOK3, _AGE80 over age bins, _RACE over race groupings, EDUCA/INCOME3/PRIMINSR over their computed bins, EXERANY2 over _TOTINDA, HAVARTH5 over _DRDXAR3, alcohol/tobacco/vaccine raws over computed indices); R7 recorded judgment calls (e.g. keep QSTLANG drop QSTVER; keep _URBSTAT drop _METSTAT; keep FLSHTMY3 as nominal with 777777/999999 → NaN; keep CHOLCHK3 as nominal because its codebook order is non-monotone; keep CHECKUP1 as ordinal with 8 = “never”; keep _STATE as a legitimate nominal predictor).

Two duplicate pairs were proven identical rather than assumed: EXERANY2 ≡ _TOTINDA (identical pairwise n = 436,659 and identical ρ = 0.2465) and _DRDXAR3 ≡ HAVARTH5 (identical n = 434,721, identical ρ = −0.2172) — both in EXP-000.

Sentinel handling: per-column value contracts, not pattern matching. The BRFSS codebook’s generic 7/9/77/99 scan is a trap, and the census’s naive scan both over- and under-counts (ADR-005): EMPLOY1 code 7 = “Retired” (131,546 rows — a valid value, only 9 is a sentinel); INCOME3 and PRIMINSR codes 7 and 9 are valid brackets/plans (sentinels are 77/99); IMFVPLA2 7/9 valid; JOINPAI2 7/9 are valid 0–10 pain ratings; _RACE 7 = Multiracial; _STATE 9 = Connecticut; _AGE80 77 = age 77 (no sentinels at all); and 6-digit sentinels 777777/999999 exist in FLSHTMY3/HIVTSTD3. Consequently every column has a codebook-exact sentinel set, asserted post-recode in src/preprocessing.py; residual-sentinel scans returned zero violations (ADR-005 sanity gates).

Day-count “none” codes and the sign-flip evidence. For day-count variables, 88 means zero days, not missing: PHYSHLTH carries 88 on 287,796 of 438,693 rows (~66%; EXP-000). The 88→0 recode is mandatory (ADR-004) and its importance is empirically measurable: on raw codes PHYSHLTH’s Spearman ρ vs y is −0.293 (EXP-000), because the “none” sentinel dominates the numeric range; after the recode it is +0.450 — the strongest whitelist feature and ultimately the model’s #1 driver (§4, §7, §9). Treating sentinels as numbers would have handed every model an inverted health signal.

Missingness after recode and imputation. Overall matrix missingness is 20.54% (ADR-005), sharply bimodal: a core-survey mode (median column missingness 5.3%) and an optional-module mode up to 74.8% (SOMALE; PREDIAB1 74.0%) (results/analysis/eda_notes.md §2). Because module assignment is questionnaire design, missingness is structural. Imputation is therefore conservative and fold-safe — always fit on training folds only inside the CV pipeline: most-frequent imputation + one-hot for nominal columns, median for ordinal/numeric/engineered (EXP-001..003 setup). For the gradient-boosting families a stronger option was verified and adopted (§6): NaN-passthrough, where the one-hot encoder encodes NaN as its own category (51 of 54 NOM columns carry NaN in matrix v1) and the boosters route NaN natively at split time — missingness stays signal instead of being blurred (EXP-005..007).

All ADR-005 sanity gates passed at matrix build: blacklist absent; EXERANY2 in / _TOTINDA out; HAVARTH5 in / _DRDXAR3 out; 437,532 valid / 72,741 positives / 16.63%; zero residual sentinels; all kept columns ≥25% effective coverage. The 89 codebook-legal _BMI5 values above 80.0 were kept and flagged (negligible for trees; 88 among non-missing valid rows in EDA).

4. Exploratory data analysis

EDA was run on matrix v1 (437,532 valid rows × 76 columns) by the data-analyst (results/analysis/eda_notes.md; figures dpi 150). It has no log row by design (analysis, not a model run); every figure was value-re-probed programmatically.

Target imbalance (Figure 1). The binary target is 16.63% positive (72,741) vs 83.37% negative (364,791), a 1 : 5.0 ratio. Stratified 10-fold CV puts ~7.3k positives in every fold, so AUC estimation is stable; the 83.4% majority-class accuracy baseline is trivially high, confirming ROC-AUC as the right metric. The figure motivated the explicit imbalance experiments of §6 rather than presuming their answer.

Missingness (Figure 2). Per-column missingness is bimodal — core survey (< 15%) vs optional modules (57–75%) — with median 5.3%, 4 fully complete columns (SEXVAR, QSTLANG, _STATE, _AGE80), 21 columns > 25% and 19 > 50% missing. Two of the strongest single predictors sit in the high-missingness regime (JOINPAI2 68.1%, POORHLTH 48.0%), so their imputed values deserve extra distrust — a fact that later shaped the NaN-passthrough choice (§6) and the interpretation of case B (§9).

Correlation structure (Figure 4). Class-conditional profiles (Figure 5). The positive class skews older (_AGE80 mean 53.7 vs 59.9 years), lower-income (median bracket 7 vs 5 of 11), functionally limited (DIFFWALK prevalence 8.9% vs 47.8% — a 5.4× rate ratio, the cleanest single explainer) and less active (EXERANY2 “any exercise” 80.2% vs 51.7%); day counts are zero-inflated with heavy right tails (PHYSHLTH mean 1.9 vs 13.8 days; MENTHLTH 3.2 vs 8.7). This epidemiologically coherent signature is itself a sanity check on the target recode. Data-quality flags recorded for modeling: _AGE80 top-coded (7.85% exactly 80), day-count zero spikes from the 88→0 recode (PHYSHLTH 65.7%, MENTHLTH 61.8% at exactly 0, shared by both classes), and deliberate 0.5 “less-than-monthly” codes in the fruit-veg items.

Block-level signal (feeds the §7 hypothesis). Mean |ρ| by EDA grouping: health days 0.316 > functional limitation/joint pain 0.306 > chronic conditions 0.167 > socio-demographics 0.144 > access 0.088 > behaviors 0.082 > diet 0.052 > admin/geo 0.035 (results/analysis/eda_notes.md §7). Current-health-status features carry roughly twice the marginal signal of chronic conditions and four times that of behaviors — but with 32–48% less coverage. This asymmetry is the empirical seed of hypothesis H1.

5. Baselines

All baselines ran on matrix v1 (all-76 features; 437,532 × 76) under the canonical folds, with imputation inside the CV pipeline. P3 summary: results/p3_baselines_summary.md.

Dummy floor (EXP-001). DummyClassifier(strategy='prior') scored ROC-AUC = 0.500000 on every one of the 10 folds (fold std 0.000000, runtime 8.3 s) — exactly what theory predicts for a constant-score predictor (AUC = ½ under all-ties). This is more than a formality: it verifies the scorer, fold builder, and target wiring, and gives every later number a measured floor.

Details worth recording: scale_pos_weight = neg/pos = 364,791/72,741 ≈ 5.0149 (stratified folds preserve the ratio); SMOTE used sampling_strategy 0.5 because a 1:1 rebalance would synthesize 262,846 rows per fold (393,778 → 656,624) vs 98,690 at 0.5 (EXP-009 note); RUS drops the per-fold majority class 328,312 → 130,932 rows (393,778 → 196,398) (EXP-010 note).

Verdict: imbalance_strategy = none for the AUC objective. Class weighting and undersampling move AUC by at most 0.00045 (≈ 0.3× fold std, both directions); SMOTE is the worst strategy on both families — the only P4 configuration where a booster loses ~2× fold std (−0.00355) and the only run of the entire zoo to lose to the plain LogReg baseline (EXP-009b, −0.00417). The reason is the metric, not the implementations: ROC-AUC is threshold-free and rank-based. Reweighting and resampling reshape the class prior and move the implicit operating point, but they do not improve the ranking of positives above negatives — which is all AUC measures. SMOTE additionally blurs the boundary with synthetic interpolated minority points (EXP-008..010 verdicts). One honest caveat is documented: EXP-009a was forced onto the impute preprocessor (SMOTE rejects NaN), so its Δ conflates strategy with preprocessing — but EXP-009b, which kept its baseline’s preprocessing, shows the damage is real for SMOTE itself. Side-finding adopted later: RUS(0.5) is AUC-neutral while 1.9× faster (73.0 vs 138.3 s for identical XGBoost; EXP-010a) — a legitimate acceleration lever for search phases, with finals always re-validated on full folds.

7. Feature selection & hypothesis

P5 asked three questions with one protocol: every selection, importance and projection is computed inside the 10 training folds only (never on full data or test rows), then scored on the canonical folds; baseline rows were never re-run (results/p5_summary.md).

Hypothesis H1 (stated before the subset experiments, coordinator-approved; EXP-012):

*The model’s predictive power concentrates in five blocks: B1 functional limitation/disability (DIFFWALK, DIFFALON, DIFFDRES, DECIDE, DEAF, BLIND); B2 health days (PHYSHLTH, MENTHLTH, POORHLTH); B3 joint pain/arthritis (JOINPAI2, LMTJOIN3, HAVARTH5, ARTHDIS2, ARTHEXER); B4 chronic conditions (DIABETE4, BPHIGH6, TOLDHI3, CHOLMED3, CVDINFR4, CVDCRHD4, CVDSTRK3, ASTHMA3, ASTHNOW, CHCSCNCR, CHCOCNCR, CHCCOPD3, ADDEPEV3, CHCKDNY2); B5 socio-demographics (_AGE80, INCOME3, EDUCA, EMPLOY1, MARITAL, _RACE, SEXVAR, _BMI5, _SMOKER3). A compact subset of these ~35 columns should match all-76 within fold noise; the complement (diet, screening, state, remaining module vars) contributes little.*

The hypothesis is grounded in EDA (§4 block ranking: health days and functional limitation carry ~2× the marginal signal of chronic conditions) rather than in post-hoc rationalization.

Basic selection: SelectKBest (mutual information) — an honest negative (EXP-011a). SelectKBest(mutual_info_classif) was fit inside each training fold on the encoded matrix; k = 50/100/200 are top-k prefixes of one per-fold MI ranking. Every k is below all-76: k=50 → 0.889178, k=100 → 0.899556, k=200 → 0.899670 (EXP-011a-50/100/200) vs 0.903355 for all-76 (EXP-006/011b). The best filter (k=200) still costs −0.0037. The mechanism: MI is univariate, so it drops correlated-but-useful columns that boosted trees exploit for free — redundancy costs a tree nothing. Selection is also unstable at small k: only 48 of 76 columns are selected in all 10 folds at k=50 (EXP-011 artifact).

9. Error analysis & XAI

Error analysis and XAI derive deterministically from the already-logged configurations EXP-014a (tuned LGBM), EXP-014b (tuned XGB) and EXP-002a (LogReg): out-of-fold (OOF) predictions come from re-fitting exactly those pipelines on the canonical folds (per-fold OOF AUCs re-verified against the logged fold scores to ≤ 4.8×10⁻⁷ for all three models; results/p7/oof_provenance.json), and SHAP values from a single full-data refit of the EXP-014a config used as the final-model proxy — a different stated object, never mixed with OOF probabilities. Authoritative interpretation note: results/analysis/error_analysis.md; computation artifacts in results/p7/.

Error taxonomy at threshold 0.5 (tuned LGBM, OOF, n = 437,532). TP/FP/TN/FN = 38,957 / 14,340 / 350,451 / 33,784 → FPR 3.93%, FNR 46.44% (TPR 0.5356, TNR 0.9607), overall error rate 11.00% (48,124 errors) at prevalence 16.63% (results/p7/error_taxonomy.csv).

The FP rate climbs monotonically toward the boundary (0.9% → 1.6% → 8.1%); the FN rate falls as health worsens (57.1% → 14.1%; class-4 TPR is 0.429 vs class-5 0.859). 42,449 of 48,124 errors (88.2%) sit exactly at the 3|4 boundary (class-3 FPs + class-4 FNs). Moreover, 41,091 rows (9.4% of all) are misclassified by all three architecturally different families simultaneously, while 86.9% are wrong in none — the errors are structured, not stochastic: if they were noise they would spread evenly across classes and models. This is the signature of boundary ambiguity in the binarized ordinal label, not model capacity: “Fair” is a heterogeneous catch-all adjacent to “Good”, and the self-rating depends on the respondent’s internal reference frame, which the 76 columns cannot observe.

Why FNR 46% vs FPR 4% is expected geometry, not a defect. The model was selected purely on ROC-AUC with no weighting or resampling (per §6) at a 1 : 5 prior. AUC optimizes ranking across all thresholds and says nothing about where the probability mass sits relative to an arbitrary 0.5 cut; with an abundant negative class the natural operating point is cheap-on-FPs, expensive-on-FNs. This is also why resampling did not help AUC in §6 — and it means 0.5 is simply the wrong knob if the model were used for screening (it is not; course project). Three archetype cases (each wrong in 3/3 model families; full vectors and z-scores in results/p7/selected_cases.json; profiles in Figure 9, local SHAP decompositions in Figures 10–12):

  • Case A — most confident false positive (row 307041, fold 4): y = 0 (GENHLTH = 3 “Good”) yet P(y=1) = 0.9595 / 0.9815 / 0.9930 (LGBM/XGB/LR). The respondent reports PHYSHLTH = 30 days (z +3.14; SHAP +1.94, the largest single contribution in any case), POORHLTH = 30 days, difficulty walking, six named chronic conditions (diabetes, heart attack, coronary disease, stroke, kidney disease, arthritis; the raw vector additionally reports hypertension and high cholesterol), BMI 51.45 (z +3.50), lowest income bracket, retired. Net: base −2.6095 + ΣSHAP +5.64 = log-odds +3.03 (proxy P = 0.954). Hypothesis: plausible label noise — response shift/adaptation, stoic response style, or survey artifact; the model faithfully reports that this measured-health vector is overwhelmingly Fair/Poor-like, and the disagreement is between the label and the respondent’s own symptom answers.
  • Case B — most confident false negative (row 392245, fold 9): y = 1 (GENHLTH = 4 “Fair”) yet P(y=1) = 0.0025 / 0.0027 / 0.0156. A clean profile on every top feature: PHYSHLTH = 0 days (SHAP −0.61, the largest push toward 0), no chronic conditions, no walking difficulty, BMI 22.14, income bracket 9/11 ($100–150k), college graduate, exercises. Every top-12 SHAP feature contributes negatively (the uniformly blue middle panel of Figure 9 is the visual statement); ΣSHAP −3.37 → log-odds −5.98. This is systematic, not an oddball: among the 50 most confident shared errors, 50/50 have PHYSHLTH ≤ 1 day, DIFFWALK = 2, DIABETE4 = 3, and 47/50 are GENHLTH = 4. Hypothesis: the error floor of the feature set — the “Fair” rating is anchored on constructs the survey does not measure (wellbeing beyond day counts, expectations, personality, social comparison); no model trained on these columns could rank this person highly without paying elsewhere.
  • Case C — the fence case (row 208256, fold 4): y = 0 (GENHLTH = 3) with LGBM P = 0.5000022 (XGB 0.598, LR 0.563); refit-proxy P = 0.481. A comorbidity block pushes toward y = 1 (DIFFWALK SHAP +0.60, DIABETE4 +0.56, heart attack +0.36, stroke +0.30, “unable to work” +0.34, near-lowest income +0.23) while current-status anchors sit near zero (PHYSHLTH = 2 days, −0.02; POORHLTH = 0, −0.03); ΣSHAP +2.53, and the waterfall is visibly two-tailed. Hypothesis: aleatoric — the population itself mixes 3s and 4s in this covariate region; P ≈ 0.5000 is the epistemically correct output, counted as an error only because thresholding must break exact ties.

Global XAI (SHAP TreeExplainer, path-dependent, raw-margin output; 20,000-row y-stratified sample, rs=42; additivity asserted to 8.8×10⁻¹⁴; one-hot contributions summed back to the 76 original columns). Top-5 mean|SHAP|: PHYSHLTH 0.6813, DIFFWALK 0.2164, DIABETE4 0.1981, _BMI5 0.1855, EDUCA 0.1786 — PHYSHLTH alone is 3.1× the #2 feature (results/p7/shap_global_importance.csv; Figure 13). Cross-method validation: SHAP ranks vs the P5 fold-internal gain ranks agree at Spearman ρ = 0.911 (p = 3.6×10⁻³⁰, n = 76; Figure 14). Two attribution mechanisms with different failure modes (gain double-counts correlated features; path-dependent SHAP spreads credit along tree paths) agreeing on the ordering is strong evidence that the driver ranking — current physical-health status first, functional limitation second, chronic-disease/SES context after — is a property of the data, and it reproduces the EDA univariate ranking (§4) and the H1 blocks (§7). The model leans on self-reported current symptoms and therefore inherits self-report bias — a feature of GENHLTH as a target, not of LightGBM.

Known caveat, stated rather than hidden: path-dependent TreeExplainer spreads credit across correlated one-hot dummies, so per-case attributions indicate which blocks drive a prediction, not precise per-feature credits (results/p7/shap_meta.json); and Case B’s missing POORHLTH still contributes −0.12 through its NaN one-hot category.

10. Conclusions & recommended model

Recommended model: the tuned LightGBM pipeline of EXP-014a — all-76 matrix v1 features, NaN-passthrough preprocessing, LGBMClassifier(n_estimators=512, learning_rate=0.0219, num_leaves=94, min_child_samples=44, subsample=0.8159, colsample_bytree=0.5980, reg_lambda=3.123, subsample_freq=1, random_state=42). Mean CV ROC-AUC 0.9043 ± 0.0017 on the canonical folds, selection-bias-free nested estimate 0.9043 ± 0.0017 (EXP-015, 0.904303; the two agree to +0.000018), significantly better than every alternative tested (Wilcoxon T1–T3, all p = 0.001953 at the n=10 floor) and the fastest of the competitive models (41.4 s per CV run untuned; 69.9 s tuned, vs RF’s 351.5 s). The recommendation rests on the full evidence chain, not one metric: whitelist-controlled data (ADR-005) → measured 0.5 floor (EXP-001) → tuned linear/tree baselines (EXP-002/003) → seven-family zoo with identical folds (EXP-004..007) → imbalance verdict “none” with a metric-level rationale (EXP-008..010) → selection/hypothesis/PCA all answered honestly (EXP-011..013) → automated search + nested CV + significance tests (EXP-014/015).

What mattered, in order of effect size. (1) Family choice: boosting over linear/bagging, +0.011 ≈ 6× fold std, significant (T3). (2) Data hygiene: the whitelist removing a proven 100%-agreement leak, plus the 88→0 recode that flipped PHYSHLTH from ρ −0.293 to +0.450 — the single largest driver (51.69% of gain). (3) Native missing-value routing: NaN-passthrough kept module missingness as signal and is part of why boosters suit this survey. (4) Tuning: real but small, +0.0010 (10/10 folds, T2) — an order of magnitude below family choice.

Honest negative results, all reported with reasons. Manual LogReg tuning was flat (the hyperparameter does not matter here); SelectKBest(MI) lost to all-76 at every k (univariate filters drop correlated-but-useful columns trees use for free); the H1-subset did not strictly match all-76 within fold noise (99.2% of lift, 0/10 folds — kept all-76, used H1 for interpretation); PCA cost 0.0146–0.0222 AUC (linear compression smears one-hot structure and forfeits NaN routing); SMOTE actively hurt (−0.004) while class weighting and undersampling were AUC-neutral — with the rank-based-metric explanation verified across two families.

Limitations. (i) Boundary ambiguity: 88.2% of errors sit at the binarized 3|4 edge; a GENHLTH=4 rater with a near-identical covariate vector to a GENHLTH=3 rater plausibly exists (Case C), so the residual error is substantially aleatoric. (ii) Self-report labels and features: both target and top predictors are self-reported; the model inherits response shift, response style and recall bias (Case A). (iii) Module missingness: the strongest univariate predictors sit in 48–68%-missing modules; imputed or NaN-routed, their signal is conditional on questionnaire design. (iv) No causal claims: this is associative prediction on a cross-sectional survey; a deployed system would additionally need calibration, subgroup fairness audits and a purpose-chosen threshold (Table 15 offers the menu). What would help: modeling GENHLTH at the ordinal level (e.g. ordinal regression / ordinal-aware objectives) instead of discarding the 1–5 ordering; richer mental-health and wellbeing constructs (the error floor of Case B is exactly the missing construct); and cost-sensitive thresholds tied to a real use case.
